# Contract periods, advance billing and recognition

Recurring agreements can bill each verified service or use an agreed monthly base rate with a 1-, 3- or 12-month billing cycle. A full cycle charges the monthly base times its selected month count. Calendar cycles start at the first of the anchor month. Anniversary cycles preserve the original day, clamping it in shorter months and restoring it afterward. A January 31 anniversary therefore advances to February 28/29 and then March 31. First/final partial periods use actual active calendar days divided by actual days in that full cycle, with integer half-up cent rounding. The supported full-cycle amount is capped at $1,000,000.

Arrears remains the default. It requires the full billing period to close, all occurrences through its active end to be generated, at least one visit, and every covered visit to be completed. Original occurrence dates govern holiday-shifted visits. A failed, cancelled or unresolved covered visit blocks billing for review. An invoice posts receivables against service revenue at period end. Free invoices are settled at zero without zero-value journal/payment rows.

Advance billing is an explicit contract option. Finance can issue coverage once its active start is reached, including an open current period, without claiming future visits as completed. The charge posts receivables against **deferred_revenue** on the actual issue date; no service revenue is recognized yet. Invoices retain immutable timing, alignment, cycle amount and coverage snapshots. Period and agreement locks, unique period keys and request hashes prevent duplicate invoices on retries or concurrent requests.

Scheduled work shows due invoices separately from closed prepaid periods awaiting verification. After the original period closes, finance can verify prepaid service. Every covered occurrence must be generated and completed. The operation transfers the remaining deferred charge to service revenue on the verification date, links the verified work to the existing invoice, and never creates a second invoice, payment or provider charge. Recognitions are append-only and one per period/invoice. Daily automation uses the same guarded operations; rerunning it does not create repeat invoices or recognition postings.

Credits reverse the remaining charge account: before recognition they reduce deferred revenue; after recognition they reduce service revenue. Any additional invoice fee revenue participates in the existing proportional credit allocation. A paid credit memo moves the excess into the customer's credit liability rather than making invoice balances negative. Before shortening prepaid coverage, finance must credit enough of the remaining contract consideration to cover the unused days. The office cannot silently discard billed coverage or initiate a refund. Started/routed future work still requires review before ending a contract.

This is full-period verification, not a daily accrual engine or an assertion of a particular accounting-standard treatment. Contract amendments, escalators, taxes and broader negotiated rate rules need separately reviewed policies. Live payment collection, production scheduled execution and accountant acceptance remain external release checks.
